tolstack, Monte Carlo tolerance analysis

SHT 03 / Python, no dependencies

Worst-case stack-up assumes every part in an assembly arrives at the wrong end of its tolerance band on the same day. It basically never happens, so designers who trust it buy tolerances they did not need. RSS is closer but lies the moment a process runs off centre.

This samples each dimension from the distribution it actually has, normal, uniform or triangular, with an optional mean shift, and reports all three methods side by side. It also ranks each dimension by its share of the output variance, which tells you the one tolerance worth money to tighten.

On the bearing seat example, worst case says the design fails outright. 200,000 sampled assemblies say 125 ppm and a Cpk of 1.17, with the housing bore owning 61 percent of the variance.

ik, planar inverse kinematics

SHT 04 / C++17, single translation unit

Two solvers. A closed-form law of cosines solution for a two-link arm that returns both the elbow-up and elbow-down answers and reports out of reach and the base dead zone as separate failures. And damped least squares for any number of links, with joint limits.

The damping is the point. Near full extension the Jacobian loses rank, a plain pseudo-inverse asks for enormous joint rates, and a real arm snaps. Levenberg-Marquardt damping bounds the joint rate instead. The solver reports manipulability as a singularity warning light.

Ten tests, including the one people skip: seeding each solve from the previous pose so a traced path stays continuous. Largest joint step over a 200-point ellipse is 0.015 rad.

profile, jerk-limited motion profiles

SHT 05 / C++17 core, Python plotter

Trapezoidal and seven-segment double-S profiles for a single axis. The trapezoid steps its acceleration instantly, so jerk is unbounded at four instants in every move. That step is a broadband input straight into the first structural mode of the machine, which is why cheap gantries ring.

The jerk-limited planner solves the geometry in closed form instead of searching, including the two cases that break naive implementations: a move too short to reach top speed, and a move too short to reach peak acceleration either.

On a 400 unit move the jerk-limited profile costs 8.4 percent more time on paper and stops exciting the structure.
